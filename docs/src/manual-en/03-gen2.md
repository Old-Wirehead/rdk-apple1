# The graphics cards: GEN2 and P-LAB

<p class="lead">In 2026, Uncle Bernie designed a color graphics card for the Apple-1, the “GEN2”, which brings the Apple II display modes to it. The emulator simulates it as an option: its soft switches, its memory, its video scan and its color screen.</p>

## Plugging in the card

The card is **unplugged by default**. There are three ways to plug it in:

- **GEN2**, in the “Graphics cards” group of the toolbar: the card is plugged in or unplugged at once; the running program carries on (press RESET if it was using the card);
- **⚙ Preferences → Uncle Bernie GEN2 colour card**, with a choice of 60 or 50 Hz;
- on the command line, `apple1-emu-gui --carte-couleur` (`--couleur-50hz`, `--sans-carte-couleur`).

On the real machine, the card has its own video output for a second monitor, in color; the Apple-1 terminal stays on its original output and works as before. The window therefore shows **two screens**: the Apple-1’s, and the **color monitor** below or beside it. A caption shows the current mode. The color monitor has its own display row: zoom, display **Normal** / **Fast** (shared with the Apple-1 screen), its own **Vintage screen** checkbox, full screen and **Detach**, to put it on a second monitor.

![The color monitor: the LORES bars of the gen2-lores.s example, and the mode caption.](img/en/gen2-moniteur.png "large")

!!! apple1 "The card is also a memory expansion"
    With the GEN2, RAM covers `$0000-$BFFF` (plus `$E000-$EFFF`). The graphics pages live in that memory: a program must not load itself there.

## The soft switches

The card reproduces the Apple II soft switches `$C050-$C057`, moved to `$C250-$C257`. They respond **to reads only**: a write has no effect. In assembly language they are therefore triggered with `BIT` or `LDA`, never with `STA`.

| Address | Name (`gen2.inc`) | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `$C250` | `SS_GR` | graphics mode |
| `$C251` | `SS_TEXT` | text mode |
| `$C252` | `SS_FULL` | full screen |
| `$C253` | `SS_MIX` | mixed: 4 lines of text at the bottom |
| `$C254` | `SS_PAGE1` | page 1 |
| `$C255` | `SS_PAGE2` | page 2 |
| `$C256` | `SS_LORES` | low resolution |
| `$C257` | `SS_HIRES` | high resolution |

{{!$C250-$C257}}

Bit 7 of a read returns **HST0**: 1 during horizontal and vertical retrace, 0 during the visible part. A program can use it to synchronize with the scan (TEST4 does). Bits 0 to 6 are floating. The card also responds on its mirrors (`$C258`, `$C3F1`…) and leaves `$C0xx` and `$C1xx` free for the ACI. RESET does not touch the switches; the emulator starts in text mode, page 1.

In Integer BASIC a read is enough too: `X=PEEK(-15792)` reads `$C250` (−15792 = `$C250` − 65536).

## Modes and memory

| Mode | Resolution | Page 1 | Page 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| TEXT | 40 × 24 characters | `$0400-$07FF` | `$0800-$0BFF` |
| LORES | 40 × 48 blocks, 16 colors | `$0400-$07FF` | `$0800-$0BFF` |
| HIRES | 280 × 192 dots, 6 colors | `$2000-$3FFF` | `$4000-$5FFF` |
| MIXED | graphics, with the last 4 lines in text | | |

**Row addresses.** As on the Apple II, rows are not consecutive in memory. Text row *r* (0 to 23) starts at:

<p class="center"><code>$0400 + (r & 7) × $80 + (r >> 3) × $28</code></p>

Each byte of the LORES page holds **two** blocks: the low nibble for the upper block, the high nibble for the lower one.

In HIRES, each scan line *y* (0 to 191) is 40 bytes of 7 dots (bit 0 on the left, bit 7 selects the palette) and starts at:

<p class="center"><code>$2000 + (y & 7) × $400 + ((y >> 3) & 7) × $80 + (y >> 6) × $28</code></p>

**LORES colors** (in the order of values 0 to 15):

| 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| black | magenta | dark blue | purple | dark green | gray | medium blue | light blue |
| **8** | **9** | **10** | **11** | **12** | **13** | **14** | **15** |
| brown | orange | gray | pink | light green | yellow | aquamarine | white |

**HIRES colors** (NTSC artifacts): with bit 7 clear, violet on even columns and green on odd ones; with bit 7 set, blue and orange; two adjacent dots lit give white. So alternating `$55`/`$2A` gives violet, `$2A`/`$55` green, `$D5`/`$AA` blue.

**Text-mode characters**: codes `$00-$3F` inverse, `$40-$7F` flashing, `$80-$FF` normal; `$E0-$FE` are lower case and `$FF` a checkerboard.

## What is approximated

The exact hues depend on NTSC, on the monitor and on the card’s trimmer capacitor: the emulator uses values close to the Apple IIgs, in the documented order. The lower-case glyphs are drawn by hand (the card’s EPROM has not been published). In HIRES, bit 7 of a byte selects the blue/orange pair instead of violet/green, and the rightmost column of a line stays orange (a test covers it); the half-dot shift that bit 7 also causes on the real card is not modelled. A mode change takes effect from the next scan line. At power-up memory is zero, which gives a screen of inverse `@` until the program fills it. The 2023 GEN1 card (at `$C050`) is not emulated.

## The P-LAB graphics card

<p class="lead">The P-LAB “Apple-1 Graphic Card” gives the Apple-1 a real video chip, the Texas Instruments <strong>TMS9918A</strong> (the one of the ColecoVision, the MSX and the TI-99/4A): 256 × 192 pixels, 16 colors, 32 sprites and a 16 KB video memory. The emulator reproduces it as an option, next to GEN2.</p>

### Plugging in the card

The card is **unplugged by default**. Click **P-LAB** in the “Graphics cards” group of the toolbar (it lights up), or choose **Plug the P-LAB card** in the **Colour screen** button when no card is plugged in, or start with `apple1-emu-gui --plab` (`--sans-plab` to remove it). The **P-LAB screen** appears as a panel (the **Colour screen** button of the Windows group shows or hides it); like every panel it can be docked, tabbed or detached into its own window.

![The P-LAB screen below the Apple-1 terminal, drawing the colour pattern of the plab-couleurs.s example. The line under the toolbar of the panel shows the display mode, the eight registers and the status.](img/en/plab.png "large")

The Apple-1 terminal keeps working as usual. The card takes the addresses `$CC00-$CFFF`: **`$CC00` is the data port** (read or write the video memory) and **`$CC01` the control port** (video address and registers on write, status on read). The emulator decodes only address line A0 inside the `$CC00-$CFFF` window, so the pair repeats over the whole 1 KB; the card’s manual does not specify this, it is an assumption (see the end of this section).

The panel has two controls and one information line:

- **CodeTank ROM…** plugs a 16 KB image into the CodeTank daughterboard: it then reads at `$4000-$7FFF`, read-only, over the RAM. Type `4000R` on the terminal to start it. **Remove ROM** takes it out;
- **NMI jumper** is the IRQ/NMI jumper of the card. Unchecked (the normal setting), the end-of-frame interrupt of the TMS9918A (60 times a second) reaches the 6502 as IRQ; checked, as NMI. The interrupt only happens when the program enables it (bit 5 of register 1);
- the **information line** shows the mode (Graphics I, Graphics II, Text, Multicolor), registers R0 to R7 in hexadecimal, the status register and the number of frames drawn.

### Programming it

The TMS9918A is reached through two ports only. To write the video memory, send the address to the control port (low byte, then high byte **or** `$40`), then write the bytes to the data port: the address increments by itself. To write a register, send the value, then the register number **or** `$80`, to the control port. Reading the status register (bit 7 = frame flag, bit 6 = fifth sprite, bit 5 = collision) clears it.

The `plab-couleurs.s` example (in the library, folder **6502**; also in `docs/exemples/en/asm/`) sets Graphics I mode, fills the pattern, color and name tables and leaves a colour pattern on the card’s screen. Run it from the editor (**▶ Run**, then `300R`, or assemble and run in one click):

```asm title="plab-couleurs.s (excerpt)"
VDATA   = $CC00
VCTRL   = $CC01

start:  ldx #0
@regs:  lda regs,x          ; R0..R7
        sta VCTRL           ; value
        txa
        ora #$80
        sta VCTRL           ; register number + $80
        inx
        cpx #8
        bne @regs
```

### Loading the public demos

The demos of the `nippur72/apple1-videocard-lib` project (written in KickC) come in four forms; the emulator knows all of them:

| File | What it is | How to load it |
|---|---|---|
| `*.bin` | raw program that starts at `$0280` | **Load** (the address dialog proposes `280`), then `280R` on the terminal |
| `*.woz` | the same, as text for the Woz Monitor | **Load**, then `280R` |
| `*.prg` | a 2-byte start address (little-endian), then the code | **Load**: the address is read from the file |
| `*_jukebox.bin` | 16 KB EPROM image for the CodeTank | **CodeTank ROM…**, then `4000R` |

Those demos are not distributed with this kit (their author has not published a licence); download them from the project’s page and keep them in your library. The emulator was checked against four of them (the demo menu with its screens and the bouncing balloon, the Amiga hand, the photo viewer and Tetris), in the RAM and in the jukebox forms.

### What is emulated, and what is not

!!! apple1 "What the emulator does"
    The four display modes (Graphics I and II, Text, Multicolor); the 16 KB video memory; the 32 sprites, 8 × 8 or 16 × 16, magnified or not, with the five-sprites-per-line limit and the collision flag; the frame flag and the end-of-frame interrupt, IRQ or NMI; the CodeTank at `$4000-$7FFF`. The TMS9918A palette is the usual one for NTSC.

!!! attention "What is not emulated"
    A register changed in the middle of a frame takes effect at the next frame (raster effects are not reproduced); the 4 KB DRAM mode; the exact timing of sprites and of video-memory accesses; the external video input; the composite-video artefacts. The state of the video chip is **not** saved in the machine states.

!!! note "Where the documentation is silent"
    The card’s manual (revision 0.4) does not say how the window is mirrored, what the bus timing is, what the video memory contains at power-up, whether the CodeTank is write-protected, or exactly how the IRQ/NMI jumper is wired. The emulator makes reasonable choices on those points (as described above); if you own the card and see a difference, please tell us.
